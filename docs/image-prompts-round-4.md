# Round four: the five new interiors

Twenty-four frames across the five interior design projects added in this round.
Each one needs a `before`, an `after` and two or three parts. Nothing else on the
site is waiting on these.

Same drop-in rule. Put the file at `src/assets/projects/<slug>/<role>.webp`,
commit, push. Nothing else to change. Until a cover arrives the index card draws
a ruled panel saying "Photography in progress" rather than a broken frame, so
these can land one at a time.

WebP quality 82. 1920x1080 for `before` and `after`, 1600x1200 for parts.

**Use the exact `part-N` number given.** The site pairs `part-1.webp` with the
first caption on that project, `part-2.webp` with the second, and so on. Getting
the number wrong silently files a photograph under someone else's caption.

## Before and after are one shot taken twice

The profile page wipes the two frames over each other on a slider. Decide the
camera position once, from the `after` description, and shoot the `before` from
exactly that spot. Same height, same focal length, same direction. Only the state
of the room changes. A mismatched viewpoint is the one mistake that cannot be
rescued in the edit.

## House style, append to every prompt

```
documentary architectural photography, shot on a 35mm full-frame camera with a tilt-shift lens, verticals corrected, natural available light, realistic materials with visible texture and slight weathering, muted neutral colour grade of white, warm grey and coffee brown, unpolished and true-to-life
```

### Interiors addendum, append to every prompt in this round

```
interior photographed in the light that is actually in the room, window light falling off across the depth of the space, practical lamps left as found, no supplementary flash, no composite exposure blending, mild shadow retained in the corners, styling minimal and evidently in use rather than staged
```

### Negative prompt

```
glossy stock-photo look, HDR, flambient blend, over-saturated sky, lens flare, plastic sheen, mirror-perfect surfaces, showroom gloss, CGI render look, fisheye distortion, leaning verticals, watermark, text, logos, cartoon, illustration, oversharpened, heavy vignette, empty staged catalogue interior
```

Every `before` carries two or three architects in hi-vis vests and hard hats,
mid-task over drawings, never looking at the camera. Overcast beats golden hour.

## 1. Harbourgate Show Residence, Richmond BC

A developer's show apartment on an upper floor, river light from a west-facing
balcony. Filed under commercial work: nobody lives here.

**`before.webp`**

```
Bare concrete shell of a new-build apartment on an upper floor, unpainted slab and soffit, capped service stubs, taped setting-out lines on the floor, plastic sheeting over the balcony opening, river and low hills visible beyond. Two interior architects in hi-vis and hard hats crouched at the future kitchen run with a rolled drawing and a laser measure, one marking the slab. Flat overcast morning.
```

**`after.webp`**

```
The same apartment completed as a show residence, photographed from the identical position and height, open living room and kitchen, pale oak floor, a marble island with a dark fault running through the slab, low wool-upholstered sofa, balcony doors open to river light and low hills. Mid-morning, cool daylight, evidently walked through rather than freshly styled.
```

**`part-1.webp`**, pairs with "Marble island"

```
Close three-quarter view of a kitchen island cut from a single slab of pale Canadian marble, a dark grey fault line running diagonally across the top and continuing down the mitred edge, brushed steel tap, one used coffee cup. Side light from a window out of frame.
```

**`part-2.webp`**, pairs with "Oak floor"

```
Low oblique detail of a wide-plank engineered oak floor in a lived-in apartment, grain and small surface scuffs visible in raking daylight, a skirting shadow gap at the wall, one chair leg entering the frame.
```

**`part-3.webp`**, pairs with "Bedroom joinery"

```
Full-height flush oak wardrobe joinery running the length of a bedroom wall, no handles, a shadow-line pull cut into each door edge, one door half open showing hanging clothes, bed edge in the foreground, soft daylight from the left.
```

## 2. Basswood Family House, Rochester NY

A 1962 split-level, opened up on the ground floor. Original terrazzo staircase
kept and made compliant rather than replaced.

**`before.webp`**

```
Interior of a 1960s split-level house mid-strip-out, load-bearing spine wall still standing and propped with adjustable steel props, plaster hacked back in patches, chalk and pencil marks for a beam position, dust sheets, original terrazzo staircase visible through the opening at the far end. Three architects in hi-vis and hard hats at the wall with a structural drawing, one reaching up to mark the plaster. Flat winter daylight from the garden windows.
```

**`after.webp`**

```
The same ground floor completed and open, photographed from the identical position, single span where the spine wall stood, dropped ceiling band marking the beam, oiled ash kitchen along the left, clay tile floor running out through open doors to a terrace, original terrazzo staircase with a steel balustrade visible at the far end. Family things on the counter, late afternoon winter light.
```

**`part-1.webp`**, pairs with "The kept staircase"

```
Original 1960s terrazzo-tread staircase with a slim painted steel balustrade, a second plain steel handrail added in front of it at a higher level, the two rails clearly separate, daylight from a landing window above, worn nosings.
```

**`part-2.webp`**, pairs with "Ash kitchen"

```
Run of oiled ash kitchen joinery, matt and open-grained rather than lacquered, a few water marks near the sink, stainless sink and a plain mixer tap, one drawer open, raking daylight from the right.
```

**`part-3.webp`**, pairs with "Lime plaster hall"

```
Hall wall in unpainted lime plaster, trowel texture and slight tonal variation across the surface, a clay tile floor below, a coat hook rail with two coats on it, soft indirect daylight.
```

## 3. Ortolan Dining Rooms, Jacksonville FL

A 1924 banking hall turned restaurant. The work is mostly acoustic and mostly
invisible.

**`before.webp`**

```
Disused 1920s banking hall before strip-out, coffered plaster vault, marble dado, terrazzo floor, stacked chairs and a rolled carpet to one side, brass teller grille still in place along one wall, a heavy steel safe door open at the far end. Two architects in hi-vis and hard hats standing in the middle of the floor, one holding an acoustic meter on a tripod and reading a clipboard. Dusty daylight from high windows.
```

**`after.webp`**

```
The same hall in service as a restaurant, photographed from the identical position and height, coffered vault overhead with perforated panels set into the coffers, the old teller counter working as a bar along the left wall with its brass grille intact, banquette seating right, carpet under the central tables, diners at half the tables, the open safe door still visible at the far end. Warm evening light from pendants and high windows.
```

**`part-1.webp`**, pairs with "Teller counter bar"

```
Detail of a 1920s marble and brass bank teller counter converted to a restaurant bar, original brass grille above, one section cut away for staff access with a clean new edge, glassware and bottles behind, scratches and patina left on the marble, warm low light.
```

**`part-2.webp`**, pairs with "Coffer panels"

```
Looking up into a coffered plaster vault, each coffer holding a matching perforated acoustic panel cut exactly to the coffer module so the ceiling reads as one continuous surface, fine perforation pattern just visible, daylight raking across from high windows.
```

**`part-3.webp`**, pairs with "Banquette run"

```
Long upholstered banquette in muted olive wool against a panelled joinery back, small round marble tables in front, one place set, terrazzo floor meeting carpet at a clean line, daylight from the right and a wall lamp above.
```

## 4. Fernbank Clinic, Toronto

A clinic fit-out on one floor, still on site. Fourteen consulting rooms and two

```
Vacant open-plan office floor stripped to the slab, raised floor removed, exposed services overhead, columns on a regular grid, full-height glazing to a city street on the south side. Three architects in hi-vis and hard hats setting out two long corridor runs in bright tape across the bare concrete, one crouched with a tape measure, one holding the plan. Flat overcast daylight.
```

**`after.webp`**

```
The same floor completed as a clinic arrival room, photographed from the identical position, warm grey linoleum floor, oak-veneered door linings, seating arranged in small separated groups rather than rows, a low reception desk to one side, full-height south glazing, two people seated apart and reading. Daylight only, calm and unclinical.
```

**`part-1.webp`**, pairs with "Arrival room"

```
Clinic waiting area with armchairs grouped in twos and threes around low tables, no row of chairs facing a door, warm grey linoleum, oak trim, a plant, daylight from a tall window to the left, one person seated with a coat over the arm of the chair.
```

**`part-2.webp`**, pairs with "Consulting room"

```
Small clinical consulting room finished in acoustic plaster with a fine matt texture, an oak-lined door, a simple desk and two chairs at ninety degrees rather than across from each other, an examination couch against the far wall, daylight from a single window, no branding.
```

**`part-3.webp`**, pairs with "Second corridor"

```
Quiet clinic corridor with oak-lined doors at intervals, warm grey linoleum, a handrail at low level, daylight entering from a glazed end wall, empty, one door standing slightly open.
```

## 5. Juniper Loft, Montreal

A top-floor garment-factory conversion. One wall, and a freestanding oak volume
doing everything else. Only two parts on this one.

**`before.webp`**

```
Bare top-floor loft in a converted early twentieth-century garment factory, original maple floor with dark oil stains where machines stood, riveted steel beams and columns, tall west-facing windows with painted frames, capped services in the floor, a stepladder and a stack of timber. Two architects in hi-vis pacing out a rectangle against the column grid, one marking the floor in chalk, the other holding a drawing. Flat overcast afternoon.
```

**`after.webp`**

```
The same loft completed, photographed from the identical position, a freestanding oak volume about two metres high standing clear of the ceiling so the riveted steel beams run over it uninterrupted, kitchen on the near face, one enclosed bedroom wall beyond, original maple floor with its oil stains left visible, tall west windows, low afternoon sun raking across the floor, a table with things on it.
```

**`part-1.webp`**, pairs with "The oak volume"

```
Freestanding oak-clad joinery volume in a loft, stopping about a metre short of the ceiling with original riveted steel beams passing above it, kitchen run on one face with an integrated worktop, a flush door on the return, daylight from the left, visible end-grain at the corner.
```

**`part-2.webp`**, pairs with "Maple floor"

```
Close raking view of an old maple factory floor, narrow boards, dark oil stains in a regular pattern where machines were bolted down, screw holes filled flush, a single chair leg and a shadow entering the frame, low afternoon light.
```
